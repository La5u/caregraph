About CareGraph

Browsing the files

Agencies opens as a paged table of every enrollment in the available files, including enrollments outside a multi-agency network. Addresses lists shared addresses and buildings, with the original CMS address and suite on expansion. Changes compares consecutive releases of the same dataset; a change between files may be a filing correction. Dates labelled first or last seen refer to CMS releases, not event dates. Certification and revocation effective dates are event dates. Revocations in Changes are observations in the published extract, rather than inferred quarterly changes.

Networks opens the list and map. Advanced contains layout and release controls, one-link people, and officers and managers; the connection finder and legend have their own collapsed controls. An agency panel can show all its listed people. The first map view keeps agencies and the owner, control, company and address nodes that join them.

What it shows

Medicare hospices and home health agencies (HHAs) grouped into networks: agencies that share an owner, a person with operational control, or a practice address in CMS ownership and enrollment files. Officers, directors and managing employees are drawn inside networks but never join two networks. Very common links are also excluded from joining: people or organizations on many enrollments (chains, consultants), holding companies on more than five enrollments, executive-suite / virtual-office addresses, and addresses with many agencies.

What it does not show

Nothing here is an allegation of fraud or wrongdoing. Shared owners and addresses are normal in health care: families, chains, management companies and shared office buildings all produce them. A link means only that a name or address appears in the listed CMS release, and links in one network or one path may date from different releases.

People are pseudonymized. The site does not show the names of people. Each person CMS lists in its owner files appears as a code such as "Person 7F3A2K9QX4M1B8RT". The code is a keyed hash (HMAC-SHA256) of the person's CMS associate ID, made with a secret that is not published, so it cannot be reversed or recomputed from a name or ID. The same code stands for the same CMS ID everywhere on the site, so a person's links to several agencies stay visible. Companies whose names identify a person (estates, trusts, or names containing a listed person's full name) are shown the same way, as "Entity …". This is pseudonymization, not anonymization: the underlying CMS files are public, and someone who looks up an agency there can see who CMS lists on it.

Identity. Each person or organization node is one CMS associate ID (the PECOS ID in the owner files), not a name. Different IDs stay separate, with one exception: two IDs with the same name are merged when both are listed on the same agency (people: same first, middle and last name, with the full middle name, not just an initial; organizations: same full name including the legal form). An incorrect or reused CMS ID can still produce an incorrect link.

Roles. The CMS owner files list owners, people with operational or managerial control, officers, directors and W-2 managing employees. A managing employee, officer or director listing does not imply ownership, and none of these listings implies wrongdoing.

Addresses are matched after standard abbreviation (STREET = ST, NORTH = N, "1711W TEMPLE" = "1711 W TEMPLE"); a number that belongs to the road name (LOOP 410, STATE ROAD 7, AVENUE U) is not read as a suite. In addition, 176 spellings of the same building (same ZIP code and house number) are merged: 130 where the street name is the same once a missing street type or the direction is ignored ("4444 CORONA" = "4444 CORONA DR"), 43 near-identical spellings ("DUBLUN" = "DUBLIN"), and 3 joined through another spelling. Spellings with different street types (DR and PL) or opposite directions (N and S) are never merged directly. Each merge was checked with the U.S. Census Bureau geocoder: 154 resolve to the same address, 5 have only one spelling in the Census data, 16 have neither and remain unverified, and in 1 (8130 Baymeadows Way W, Jacksonville) the Census matched one spelling to a nearby street although both spellings name the same street. An address node lists its merged spellings, and each link shows the spelling CMS used. Names and addresses are displayed in title case for readability; CMS lists them in capitals, and the link records and source rows show them exactly as CMS spells them. A shared address joins two agencies only if CMS lists both there in at least one common release; an agency listed at the same address only before or after the other (a later tenant of the same suite, for example) is shown at the address but not joined through it. Addresses without a suite number that were checked to be multi-tenant office buildings, and shared office centers whose tenants all use the center's suite number, are shown but do not join networks. A building whose agencies use different suites gets its own building node, labeled with how many agencies CMS has listed there; different suites are usually separate tenants, so building nodes do not join networks either. Revoked agencies are shaded by the reason in the CMS Revocation Extract: abuse of billing privileges (42 CFR 424.535(a)(8)), affiliation ((a)(19)), or other grounds. A matched practice address may be a residence or shared premises and does not establish a personal relationship or shared control.

"Not in latest file" means the enrollment is missing from the newest CMS enrollment release and has no revocation row. It does not mean the agency closed. "Revoked" repeats the CMS Revocation Extract of 2026-10-21; the reason shown is the CMS reason code text. A revocation is a CMS administrative action, not a criminal finding; it can be appealed and may later be reversed, and a revocation of an agency is not a finding against any person listed on it. Check the current CMS data before relying on it.

Data

The code that builds this site, and the indicator backtest, are open source: github.com/La5u/caregraph (MIT). The data is not in it; it is all downloaded from the sources below.

  • CMS Hospice All Owners and Hospice Enrollments, quarterly releases 2023-03-31 to 2026-09-29
  • CMS Home Health Agency All Owners, quarterly releases 2023-03-31 to 2026-09-29, and HHA Enrollments, quarterly releases 2023-03-31 to 2026-09-29
  • CMS Revoked Medicare Providers and Suppliers (Revocation Extract), 2026-10-21
  • CMS Post-Acute Care Utilization by Provider, CY2024 (payments, patients; for agencies without 2024 claims, the latest of CY2021-2023 is shown with its year): hospice, home health
  • CMS Care Compare hospice data (certification date, Hospice Care Index, live discharges), Aug 2026
  • NPPES NPI Registry (authorized official), Oct 2026

All sources are public U.S. government data. Every link lists the CMS records behind it: one entry per distinct row (role, ownership %, association date), with every quarterly release it appears in. Each release links to the matching rows in the CMS data API (JSON), filtered by enrollment ID, so anyone can check them at the source. Those CMS rows show the names of everyone listed on the agency. Agency fields link to their public source: the Care Compare page for the CCN, the NPPES page for the NPI, the revocation row, and the payment row. The data may contain errors from the source files or from address and ID matching. A missing "revoked" marker does not mean an agency was never sanctioned: only the CMS Revocation Extract is checked.

Report suspected fraud

This site does not take or forward tips. If you have information about Medicare or Medicaid fraud, waste or abuse, report it directly to the government:

  • HHS Office of Inspector General hotline: online form, or 1-800-HHS-TIPS (1-800-447-8477), TTY 1-800-377-4950.
  • Medicare.gov: reporting Medicare fraud and abuse, or 1-800-MEDICARE (1-800-633-4227), for questions about specific Medicare claims.
  • State Medicaid Fraud Control Units, for Medicaid fraud and for abuse or neglect of patients in care facilities.

Include the agency name, its CCN or NPI, and what you observed. A network or link on this site is not by itself evidence of fraud.

Indicators

A prompt to look closer, not a finding. These were tested against later CMS revocations; most agencies with an indicator have done nothing wrong. No check shows that any agency did anything wrong. Indicators apply to hospices only, never to people, companies or addresses, and there is no combined score.

How they were tested. Hospices with Medicare claims in 2023 and a CCN in the January 2024 enrollment file, not revoked before, were followed to see which were later revoked by CMS (any revocation in the CMS Revocation Extract effective after the data; for Care Compare measures, which cover 2023-24, only revocations from 2025). A hospice is flagged when its value is at or beyond the national 95th percentile (or 5th, for low values) of hospices, or by the fixed rule shown. The cut-offs were set before the results were seen and were not tuned. A check was kept only if hospices with it were later revoked more often than others in the same state (Mantel-Haenszel risk ratio, lower 95% limit above 1, at least 5 revoked hospices with it). The building check was added after the first run.

What did not hold up. Live discharge rates, a small daily census, fast payment growth, and every home health check did not hold up; they are shown as plain metrics with national percentiles only.

Limits. A revocation is a biased stand-in: CMS finds what it looks for, and most improper billing never leads to a revocation. The rates above are from the same hospices the checks were chosen on. Metrics come from the CMS Medicare Post-Acute Care and Hospice file by provider (2024) and Care Compare hospice measures (2023-24); a value CMS did not publish is shown as "not assessed", never as zero.

Privacy

Who is responsible. This site is published by Lasu Shin, a data scientist in France, acting as a private individual and as the controller for the personal data it contains. Contact: lasushin@proton.me.

What personal data, and from where. Pseudonymized records of people listed in the public CMS Hospice and Home Health Agency All Owners files (their role on an agency, ownership percentage, the association date CMS reports, and the CMS release dates they were listed in). No names, CMS IDs, contact details or other identifiers of people are published. The data is not collected from the people concerned. Agencies are shown with the name and practice address CMS publishes for them; for a small agency these can relate to its owner. Phone numbers are not shown. Because CMS publishes no contact details for these people, they are not told individually; this notice informs them (Article 14(5)(b) GDPR).

Why, and on what basis. To help the public, journalists and oversight bodies understand who owns and runs Medicare hospices and home health agencies, a sector with documented problems of hidden ownership. The legal basis is legitimate interest (Article 6(1)(f) GDPR), limited by pseudonymization, by leaving out people's names and identifiers, and by keeping the site out of search engines.

Who receives it. Anyone who visits the site. The site sets no cookies, uses no analytics and loads nothing from third parties; Cloudflare, its host, may receive network error reports from browsers. Your browser's local storage keeps only your display choices and any case notes you make; they never leave your browser.

Hosting. Cloudflare, Inc. (101 Townsend St, San Francisco, CA 94107, USA, +1 650 319 8930) hosts and delivers the whole site, and processes visitors' network data (such as IP addresses) to do so, as a processor under its data processing addendum. Cloudflare is in the United States; transfers rely on its certification under the EU-U.S. Data Privacy Framework and the EU standard contractual clauses in that addendum. The publisher's identity and address have been given to Cloudflare (as the French law n° 2004-575 for confidence in the digital economy, LCEN, requires of non-professional publishers).

How long. Each data update replaces the previous one. A person stays on the site while CMS lists them on any hospice or home health agency in a release from the last six years, the period in which U.S. False Claims Act cases can generally be brought; after that they are dropped from the next update. Data about visitors is kept by Cloudflare only as long as needed to deliver and protect the site.

Assessment. The balancing test, impact assessment and record of processing are published with the source code.

Your rights. You can ask for access, rectification, erasure or restriction, and you can object to the processing (Articles 15 to 21 GDPR), by writing to the address above. Entries removed on request stay removed in later updates. Requests are answered within one month. You can also complain to the CNIL (cnil.fr/plaintes). Corrections to the underlying records must be made with CMS.

Corrections

If something is wrong, please report it to lasushin@proton.me, with the network number and the agency, code or link concerned. Requests to correct or remove an entry are answered there. Corrections to the underlying records must be made with CMS (PECOS).
